3. The G-Spot: Location & Function

Anatomical Location

The G-spot, also called the Gräfenberg spot (named after the gynecologist Ernst Gräfenberg who first documented it), is located on the anterior (front) wall of the vagina, approximately 1-2 inches inside the vaginal opening. It's positioned along the urethra, which is surrounded by the urethral sponge tissue.

Finding Your G-Spot:

1. Insert one or two fingers into the vagina, palm facing upward

2. Curl your fingers in a "come here" motion toward the front wall

3. Feel for an area that may be slightly rougher in texture, about the size of a small bean to a quarter

4. It may take time to locate – patience and relaxation are essential

What It Feels Like

The G-spot typically feels textured or bumpy compared to surrounding tissue. Some describe it as having a subtle ridge or elevation. When properly stimulated, it can swell slightly and become more pronounced.

G-Spot: Function & Response

13. Safety & Health Considerations

Hygiene Practices

Hand Hygiene: Wash hands thoroughly before and after any intimate activity. Trim and smooth fingernails completely to avoid tissue tears.

Medical Considerations

Certain medical conditions may affect comfort or require adjustments:

• Prostate Issues: Those with prostate problems should consult their healthcare provider before engaging in prostate play

Pain vs. Discomfort

Initial sensations might feel unusual but shouldn't be painful. Sharp pain indicates something is wrong. Stop immediately if you experience:

• Sharp or shooting pain

• Burning sensations (beyond initial adjustment)

• Difficulty with normal bathroom functions afterward
